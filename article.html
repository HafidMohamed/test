Inside the Yirgacheffe Valley: How Ethiopia's Most Famous Coffee is Grown

Ethiopia is the birthplace of coffee. It is here, in the southwestern highlands, that Coffea arabica first grew wild thousands of years ago. And nowhere in Ethiopia is coffee more revered — or more extraordinary — than in Yirgacheffe.

Last October, I travelled to the Gedeo Zone in southern Ethiopia to spend two weeks with the farmers who grow the beans we roast every week here in Kreuzau. What I found was not just exceptional coffee, but a way of life built entirely around it.

The Geography of Yirgacheffe

Yirgacheffe sits at elevations between 1,700 and 2,200 metres above sea level. At this altitude, the coffee cherry matures slowly — sometimes taking nearly a year from flower to ripe fruit. This extended development period concentrates sugars and complex flavour compounds in ways that simply cannot be replicated at lower altitudes.

The soil in Yirgacheffe is a deep, reddish clay that is rich in organic matter. Generations of coffee trees have fallen and decomposed here, creating a natural mulch that feeds the new growth. The local farming system — called garden coffee — integrates coffee trees into a multi-storey forest structure alongside banana plants, false banana (enset), and native shade trees.

Meeting the Farmers

I met Tesfaye Bekele at his family's small plot just outside the town of Kore. His family has been growing coffee on this land for four generations — and the trees show it. Some of the older plants are over 80 years old, their trunks thick as my arm, their canopy reaching five metres into the air.

"My grandfather planted these trees. They give the best cherries because they have deep roots that reach the water below the clay. Young trees cannot do this."

— Tesfaye Bekele, fourth-generation coffee farmer, Kore, Ethiopia

Tesfaye and his wife Mekdes hand-pick every cherry. There are no machines here — each cherry is checked individually, and only those at peak ripeness are selected. A skilled picker can harvest around 150 kilograms of ripe cherry per day. It takes roughly 7–8 kilograms of fresh cherry to produce 1 kilogram of green bean.

Processing: Washed vs. Natural

After harvesting, the cherries are taken to a central washing station — a cooperative facility shared by dozens of small farmers in the area. It is here that the coffee undergoes its first transformation.

What is washing station processing?

In washed processing, the fruit pulp is removed from the bean within hours of harvest, and the beans are fermented in water tanks for 24–72 hours to remove the remaining mucilage layer. The beans are then washed with clean water and dried on raised beds in the sun. This produces a clean, bright cup that highlights the bean's inherent character.

Our Ethiopian Yirgacheffe uses the washed process, which gives it its characteristic clarity and brightness. Natural-process coffees from the same region can be excellent, but the washed version better expresses the terroir — the sense of place — that makes Yirgacheffe so distinctive.

What Makes Yirgacheffe Unique

When I brew our Yirgacheffe here in Kreuzau, I am always struck by how it tastes nothing like a "typical" coffee. The aroma alone is transformative — bergamot, jasmine, fresh lemon. The flavour follows: blueberry and stone fruit, a long floral finish, and a brightness that I can only describe as effervescent.

  • Aroma: bergamot, jasmine, lemon verbena
  • Flavour: blueberry, peach, apricot, dark grape
  • Acidity: bright, juicy, citrus-forward
  • Body: medium-light, silky
  • Aftertaste: long floral finish with hints of earl grey tea

How to Brew It at Home

To get the most out of Ethiopian Yirgacheffe, we recommend a pour-over method that allows those delicate notes to develop without being masked by milk or sugar. Here are our recommended parameters:

Recommended brewing parameters (V60):

Coffee:        15g
Water:         250ml at 93°C
Grind size:    Medium-fine (e.g. Comandante 22–24 clicks)
Brew time:     2:45 – 3:15 minutes
Bloom:         45ml for 30 seconds

💡 Tip: Ethiopian coffees are particularly sensitive to over-extraction. If your cup tastes harsh or astringent, try a slightly coarser grind or reduce your water temperature by 1–2°C.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes Yirgacheffe coffee so special? ▼

Yirgacheffe beans are grown at extremely high altitude (1,700–2,200m) in ancient forest gardens. The combination of altitude, fertile soil, and traditional cultivation produces complex floral and fruity notes rarely found in other origins.

Is Ethiopian coffee naturally processed or washed? ▼

Both methods are used in Ethiopia. Natural-process coffees develop intensely fruity, wine-like flavours. Washed coffees from the same region tend to be cleaner and brighter, highlighting floral and citrus notes.

What brewing method works best for Ethiopian Yirgacheffe? ▼

Pour-over methods like V60 or Chemex are ideal because they allow the delicate floral and fruity notes to shine through. We recommend a water temperature of 92–94°C and a medium-light grind for best results.
